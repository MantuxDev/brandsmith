# Naming

Goal: a shortlist of names that fit the strategy and survive basic screening, with the legal checks clearly handed
to a human.

## 1. Naming brief

Confirm before generating: what's being named (company, product, sub-brand), markets and languages, the
personality, must-haves (length, language, a word to include/avoid), and the naming **architecture** (does it need
to sit next to existing brand names?).

## 2. Name types

| Type | Example pattern | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | says what it does | Instantly clear | Hard to protect, generic |
| Suggestive | evokes a benefit/feeling | Memorable, protectable | Needs context |
| Metaphorical | borrowed word, new meaning | Rich story, distinctive | Can feel arbitrary |
| Invented / coined | new word | Highly protectable, domain-friendly | Needs investment to mean something |
| Compound / blend | two roots combined | Explains + distinctive | Can feel forced |
| Founder / place | person or place name | Heritage, authenticity | Limits scale, personal |
| Acronym | initials | Short | Meaningless, forgettable unless already known |

## 3. Generate

Generate broadly (40–80 candidates across types), then cut hard to 8–12. Group by type and by the strategic idea
each one expresses. Show the reasoning for each shortlisted name in one line.

## 4. Screen (what you can do)

For each shortlisted name, check and report:
- **Fit** with positioning and personality (1–5).
- **Sound & spelling** — easy to say once heard, spell once seen? Ambiguous pronunciation across target languages?
- **Linguistic check** — meaning or unfortunate associations in the target markets' languages (and the major ones
  likely in future). Be explicit about your confidence; recommend a native-speaker check for anything important.
- **Length & visual shape** — letter count, ascenders/descenders, potential for a good wordmark or monogram.
- **Category clutter** — does it sound like known competitors?
- **Obvious conflicts** you're aware of (famous brands with the same or similar name).

## 5. Checks for a human (always state these)

You cannot reliably verify these; list them as next steps for the designer or client:
- Trademark search in the relevant registers and classes (e.g. EUIPO, USPTO, WIPO, national offices) — ideally
  by a trademark attorney before any investment.
- Domain availability (and key TLDs), social handles.
- Company register conflicts in the operating countries.

Record the shortlist in `research/naming.md` and the final pick, with checks done by whom, in `decisions.md`.
