# Research, conventions & directions

Goal: know the category well enough to decide where to conform (for recognition) and where to break (for
distinction). Output in `research/`.

## 1. Competitor visual audit

For 6–12 brands (direct competitors, the category leader, one or two aspirational outsiders):

| Brand | Logo type | Colour(s) | Type style | Imagery | Voice | Tagline | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Work from public sources only (websites, social, packaging photos). Cite URLs.
- If you can browse, collect the material; if not, ask the designer for screenshots or links and work from those.
- Never put competitor logo files into the deliverables; they're for analysis and the shelf test only.

Save as `research/competitor-audit.md`.

## 2. Conventions map

From the audit, summarise:
- **Category codes** — what the category does visually and verbally (e.g. fintech: blue, geometric sans,
  abstract marks, "simple / secure"; craft coffee: kraft, stamps, serif or hand-drawn, origin stories).
- **Codes to keep** — what buyers need in order to recognise the category (breaking these can cost trust).
- **Codes to break** — clichés where difference is cheap and memorable.

A simple 2×2 positioning map helps (choose axes that matter for this category: e.g. traditional ↔ progressive,
mass ↔ niche, warm ↔ technical). Plot competitors; mark the white space. Make it a quick SVG or HTML file.

## 3. White space

State the opportunity in one or two sentences: "Everyone is cold and blue and technical; nobody is warm and
human while still feeling expert." Confirm with the designer → `brand.md` → Market → White space.

## Directions

Before drawing a logo, align on 2–3 **visual directions** (also called territories). Each direction:

- **Name** — two or three words ("Quiet Precision", "Field Notes", "Loud Optimism").
- **Idea** — one sentence linking it to the strategy.
- **Keywords** — 5–7 adjectives.
- **Palette sketch** — 3–5 colours (run `color_tools.py swatch` to make an HTML swatch).
- **Type mood** — category of type (grotesk, humanist, transitional serif, slab, mono, display) with 1–2 example
  families, available and licensable.
- **Imagery** — what photography/illustration would look like.
- **Reference list** — descriptive (e.g. "Swiss poster typography, 1960s"), not other brands' logos.
- **Risk** — what could go wrong with this direction.

Directions should be **genuinely different** from each other (one safe, one stretch, one in between is a good
spread). Save to `research/directions.md`, optionally a moodboard HTML page with the palette, type specimens
(from Google Fonts or system fonts) and keywords.

Checkpoint: the designer picks or merges a direction before logo work.
