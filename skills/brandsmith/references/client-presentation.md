# Client, presentation & revisions

Goal: help the designer sell good work, manage feedback rounds, and keep scope under control.

## 1. Presenting concepts

Structure of a concept presentation (deck or page):

1. **Recap** — the brief and strategy in the client's words (they agreed to it; it's the yardstick).
2. **Insight** — the white space / the one thing.
3. **Each concept** — name, idea in one sentence, the mark big and alone, then in context (3–6 applications),
   then how it answers the brief. One concept per section; never side-by-side first.
4. **Recommendation** — which and why, tied to the brief.
5. **Next steps** — what decision is needed, what happens after.

Rationale writing: tie every visual choice to a strategic reason ("the open counter echoes your open-door policy"
is weak if forced; "a single, heavy weight because the brand will mostly live as a 40 px app icon" is strong).
Avoid post-rationalised symbolism the client will see through.

Offer to draft speaker notes and anticipate the three objections the client is most likely to raise, with answers.

## 2. Feedback rounds

- Collect feedback in writing; log it in `.brand/feedback.md` (verbatim → interpretation → action → status).
- Translate vague feedback with the table in `discovery.md` §4.
- Separate: **changes within scope** (refinement), **new direction requests** (may be a new round), **personal taste
  from one stakeholder** (check against the brief and the decision-maker).
- Prepare the designer's reply: what we'll change, what we recommend keeping and why, what's out of scope.

## 3. Rounds and scope

- Track rounds in `status.md` against what the proposal included (e.g. "3 concepts, 2 refinement rounds").
- When a request exceeds scope, help the designer phrase it neutrally: what's included, what the request adds,
  options (swap, extend, quote).

## 4. Scoping and estimating (optional)

When asked, help draft a scope: phases, deliverables per phase, rounds included, timeline, assumptions,
exclusions (e.g. trademark search, photography, print management, web build). Estimate effort in days per phase
from the designer's own rates and pace; never invent market prices as fact.
