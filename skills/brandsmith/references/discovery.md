# Discovery & brief

Goal: a brief the designer can design against and defend to the client. Output goes into `.brand/brand.md` → Brief,
Audience, Market.

## 1. Client questionnaire

Offer to generate a questionnaire the designer sends to the client (file: `research/client-questionnaire.md`, in
the client's language). Tailor it: drop questions already answered, add sector-specific ones. Keep it to
what the client can actually answer in 20–30 minutes. Base set:

**Business**
1. In one sentence, what do you do and for whom?
2. Why are you doing this project now? What prompted it?
3. Where do you want the business to be in 3 years?
4. What do customers say when they recommend you?

**Audience**
5. Who is your ideal customer? Describe a real one.
6. What problem do they have before they find you? What alternatives do they consider?
7. Why do they choose you — and why do some choose someone else?

**Market**
8. Who are your main competitors? Which brands (any sector) do you admire, and why?
9. What makes you genuinely different? What could a competitor not credibly say?

**Brand**
10. Three words you want people to use about you. Three words you never want to hear.
11. Is there anything from the current brand that must stay (name, colour, symbol, phrase)?
12. Where will the brand appear most? (shop, app, packaging, social, B2B decks, vehicles…)

**Practical**
13. Deliverables, deadline, budget range, who approves.
14. Any constraints: existing materials, legal, sector rules, languages/markets.

## 2. Workshop exercises (for a live session)

Give the designer a short facilitation plan when asked. Pick the 2–4 that fit the time available:

- **Personality sliders** — pairs on a 1–5 scale: classic ↔ modern, serious ↔ playful, exclusive ↔ accessible,
  quiet ↔ loud, refined ↔ raw, local ↔ global, corporate ↔ personal, rational ↔ emotional. Record each score;
  disagreements among stakeholders are the most useful output.
- **We are / we are not** — "We are confident, not arrogant." Five pairs.
- **Brand as a person / place / car** — projective questions that surface tone quickly.
- **Archetype card sort** — the 12 archetypes as cards; pick primary + secondary (see `strategy.md`).
- **Visual card sort** — 30–40 images from diverse aesthetics; "like / dislike / why". Build from it, not from
  competitor logos.
- **Obituary / headline test** — "What would the industry say if you disappeared tomorrow?" / "The headline
  about you in 5 years".

## 3. Writing the brief

Synthesise into `brand.md` → Brief. A good brief:
- fits on one page;
- names **one** primary audience;
- states the problem to solve (not the deliverable list);
- has a **single-minded proposition** ("the one thing");
- lists mandatories and no-gos;
- defines success (how will we know the identity works?).

Flag gaps and contradictions back to the designer (e.g. "premium positioning but budget for one concept round").

## 4. Translating vague feedback

Clients and stakeholders give feedback in feelings. Translate it into design hypotheses and confirm with the
designer before acting. Common translations (always as hypotheses, not rules):

| They say | Often means | Levers to try |
|---|---|---|
| "More modern" | Looks dated vs their reference set | Simplify, reduce detail, open spacing, cleaner sans, flatter colour, lighter weight or tighter geometry |
| "Make it pop" | Low contrast or low distinctiveness | Increase value contrast, a saturated accent, bigger scale contrast, fewer competing elements |
| "More premium" | Feels busy or cheap | Restraint: fewer colours, more space, refined serif or thin sans, tighter craft, darker or muted palette |
| "Too cold / corporate" | Lacks warmth or human signal | Warmer hues, rounded terminals, humanist type, conversational copy, imagery of people |
| "Too childish" | Over-rounded, oversaturated, toy-like | Reduce roundness, desaturate, more weight contrast, less bounce in motion |
| "Not us" | Personality mismatch | Revisit sliders; ask which reference brand feels more "them" and why |
| "I don't like the colour" | Personal taste or a real association | Ask what it reminds them of; check category codes and cultural meaning before changing |
| "Can you make the logo bigger?" | Hierarchy/visibility worry | Check the layout hierarchy and minimum sizes; the fix is rarely the logo size |

Log each point in `.brand/feedback.md` with the interpretation and the action.
