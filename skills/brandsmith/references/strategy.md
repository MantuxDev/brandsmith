# Brand strategy

Goal: decisions that make design and copy choices obvious. Output into `.brand/brand.md`.

Designers vary: some own strategy fully, some receive it from a strategist or the client. Ask which. If strategy
exists, evaluate it with rubric E (`rubrics.md`) and only fill gaps.

## 1. Positioning statement

```
For [primary audience] who [need / tension],
[Brand] is the [frame of reference / category]
that [key benefit],
because [reason to believe].
Unlike [main alternative], it [point of difference].
```

Write 2–3 variants that make different strategic bets, recommend one, and say what each bet sacrifices. Test:
- Could a competitor sign it? → too generic.
- Is the benefit something the audience cares about, in their words?
- Is the reason to believe true and provable?

## 2. Personality

- 3–5 traits, each with a **"…but not…"** guardrail (confident but not arrogant).
- Personality sliders from discovery, if run.
- Optional archetype (primary + secondary). Use as shorthand for tone, not as a straitjacket:

| Archetype | Core drive | Visual tendencies | Voice tendencies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Innocent | Simplicity, optimism | Light, airy, soft palette, simple shapes | Plain, warm, honest |
| Everyman | Belonging | Unpretentious, familiar, friendly type | Down-to-earth, inclusive |
| Hero | Mastery, courage | Bold, strong contrast, dynamic forms | Direct, motivating |
| Outlaw | Disruption | Raw, high contrast, unconventional type | Provocative, irreverent |
| Explorer | Freedom, discovery | Earthy or vast, rugged textures | Adventurous, curious |
| Creator | Innovation, expression | Distinctive, crafted, expressive | Imaginative, precise |
| Ruler | Control, stability | Classic, structured, rich dark palette | Authoritative, measured |
| Magician | Transformation | Mysterious, luminous, transformative motifs | Visionary, evocative |
| Lover | Intimacy, pleasure | Sensual, rich colour, elegant curves | Warm, sensory |
| Caregiver | Service, protection | Soft, rounded, reassuring | Gentle, supportive |
| Jester | Joy, play | Bright, playful, rule-breaking | Witty, light |
| Sage | Truth, understanding | Clean, considered, serif or rational sans | Knowledgeable, clear |

## 3. Messaging hierarchy

- **Promise** — the one thing (often the seed of the tagline).
- **Pillars** — three supporting themes; each with a headline, a sentence and 2–3 proof points.
- **Proof points** — facts, numbers, credentials, testimonials. Mark any claim needing substantiation.
- **Elevator pitch** — 1 sentence, 30 words, 100 words.

## 4. From strategy to design brief

Close the strategy with a short **creative bridge**: for each personality trait, what it implies visually and
verbally (e.g. "precise → tight geometry, consistent stroke, no decorative flourishes; short declarative copy").
This is what the logo and identity work will be judged against.
