# Applications & touchpoints

Goal: prove the system works where the brand actually lives, and give the designer presentation-ready mockups.

## 1. Pick the touchpoints that matter

From `brand.md` → Audience → key touchpoints. Prioritise the 4–6 most frequent or most important. Typical sets:

| Business type | Priority touchpoints |
|---|---|
| Digital product / SaaS | App icon, website hero, product UI header, social avatar + post, email header, pitch deck slide |
| Hospitality / food | Signage, menu, cup/packaging, bag, social post, staff uniform |
| Retail / consumer goods | Packaging front, shelf view, label, e-commerce product page, social ad |
| Professional services | Business card, letterhead, website, LinkedIn banner, proposal cover, email signature |
| Culture / events | Poster, ticket, social campaign, wayfinding, merchandise, motion title |

## 2. Mockups you can build

Build simple, honest mockups as HTML/SVG using the real assets and tokens (no photoreal fakery that hides problems):
- Business card front/back (85×55 mm or 3.5×2 in) at 1:1 scale on screen.
- Social avatar (circle crop) and post templates (1:1, 4:5, 9:16).
- Website hero and header at desktop and mobile widths.
- App icon on light/dark home screens.
- Email signature, letterhead, slide title and content templates.
- Simple packaging front or label flat.

For photoreal mockups (signage, merchandise, packaging in situ), recommend the designer's own mockup tools and give
them the exact assets and specs.

## 3. Templates

For touchpoints others will produce repeatedly (social posts, slides, documents), propose templates with fixed
zones (logo position, headline area, image area) and rules. Record them as part of the guidelines.

## 4. Evaluate the set

Score the set with rubric B and the consistency audit (rubric D). Fix what breaks before guidelines.
